AI-DE: understand the work, review the decisions

Exploratory proposal, not an accepted specification. The user's platform decision is settled: AI-DE remains a local desktop workbench. The HTML is an interactive review artifact; it is not a proposal to replace the product with a hosted website. No production change is made in this work.

Opportunity and recommendation

AI-DE already has a local daemon, durable fact store, graph/tree exploration, static UML views, governed agent conversations, compilation history, dispatch receipts and watcher evidence. Its primary orientation is sessions and perspectives; the requested factory needs a durable connection from accepted intent → implementation → assertion → executed proof → human decision. This is an evidence operating model, not another chat box or a more elaborate terminal.

Recommendation, Inferred: make accepted work items the default focus. Offer three stable destinations: Factory, Review, Understand. Put PR/test/rigor lenses inside Review; graph/architecture lenses inside Understand. Share project, scope, selection and the evidence drawer. Keep conversations, code, full diffs and terminal output one deliberate drill-down away. Six user jobs do not need six independent applications or six competing dashboards.

Evidence and limits

Verified local baseline: 49 capability rows are inventoried; the normal App build produces all four primary assemblies with no warnings/errors; 57 selected Core tests pass. Two focused probes reproduce stale same-revision indexing and a response that exceeds its frame cap. The forensic backlog contains 11 proposed findings, including those two P1 issues. This is not a whole-test-suite, native accessibility, package release or provider-qualification sign-off. See review, inventory, and proof.

Verified primary-source patterns: plans and artifacts, goal supervision, semantic walkthroughs, review snapshots and progressive detail are now documented by the named vendors. Their hosting models and quality claims are not automatically transferable. The new Claude project coordinator and ultrareview are cloud-scoped; Grok's plan edit gate does not cover shell writes/subagents. Current Copilot preview approvals differ from Anthropic's advisory review service. Research and 42-source ledger.

Flagged: no universal confidence, lines-changed or coverage threshold proves that a human can safely be removed. Start conservatively and calibrate by local escaped defects, reversibility, reviewer decisions and cost. The mockup's metrics, work items and PR are visibly synthetic.

Audience and core scenario

The primary user is a technically expert owner or lead directing agents across a repository. They need to know what changed, what is proven, what is risky and where their judgment matters. A reviewer needs a smaller evidence packet without losing the ability to inspect everything. An investigator needs scoped graph/UML/code navigation without a global hairball.

Core scenario: open a work item; review its accepted obligations and architecture constraints; inspect the proposed execution plan; deliberately authorize work; receive a behavior-based change summary; review only the decisions requiring human judgment while retaining full-diff access; inspect test and drift evidence; accept or reject the exact change baseline with reasons. New content or changed intent makes relevant evidence and decisions stale.

Prior assessments and decision events remain append-only.

Journey 1 — Spec-driven development and progress

Factory opens an attention list, not a wall of progress bars: unresolved intent, missing proof, changed contracts and work ready for review. Select an outcome to see its Spec, Plan, Runs and Evidence. Acceptance criteria show expected behavior, its authority and proof. An empty proof row says “No evidence linked”; it does not silently count as incomplete code or as a passing criterion. Derived progress separates built, executed, reviewed and accepted.

Accepting a plan is distinct from authorizing tools, and both differ from accepting a change. A run can be finished while its outcome remains blocked by missing evidence or a human decision. The conversation and Console remain contextual children of that work item, with existing low-level controls available for experienced operators. AI-Forward's knowledge/spec/architecture/design/ implementation/proof sequence becomes a visible chain of artifacts, not mandatory modal ceremony for every trivial change. Existing proportionality and veto floors remain.

A document-style authoring studio and a real prompt stack

The author's normal surface should be a prompt document, not a terminal input line. The user's addition explicitly asks for rich text, multi-edit-style editing, a stack to move backward/forward through earlier prompts, and a credible migration from today's implementation. These remain inside Factory and the selected work item; they do not add another top-level application.

Distinguish three editing capabilities: multi-cursor text editing within a block; structured blocks for goal, criteria, constraints, examples and context; and scoped batch edits with a match preview and undo. They solve different tasks. The prototype implements rich bold/emphasis, acceptance/code insertion, keyboard-reachable block movement, previewed exact-match replacement and an undoable editor operation. It does not claim a full CodeMirror multi-cursor implementation.

The prompt stack is not text undo or the execution transcript. Back/Forward preserves the current scratch copy; historical submissions remain immutable; reusing or branching an earlier prompt creates a new draft identity. Show title, source work item, status, parent and position. Compare earlier intent, constraints and context against the current canonical text. Navigation never sends, replays an uncertain delivery or silently changes the target provider/worktree.

Keep one editable source model with two projections: rich document/text-block editing and the exact canonical Markdown/typed payload that will be sent. A context chip may identify a spec clause, graph node, diagram, code range, test run or prior answer at an exact version. Mention, attach and grant write scope are distinct. Do not expand a reference into private source or grant permissions because it appears in a rich editor. Prepared output becomes stale after material edits or changed context. The existing host-owned compiler/send gate remains authoritative.

Journey 2 — PR analysis and productive human review

Review begins with “What needs your judgment?” and why. A packet groups semantic changes by behavior or architecture boundary, shows a before/after scenario and affected sequence, then production code, changed tests, actual runs, counter-evidence and residual uncertainty. Review by requirement or changed behavior rather than alphabetical files.

Every packet names exact base/head/content identity, inspected scope, omissions and analyzer failures. A tour shows what it covered and offers the complete diff and impacted dependencies. Human actions record fixed, false positive, intentional, deferred or needs decision with a reason. Do not turn dismissed comments into a correctness score.

Policy initially requires human judgment for credentials/authorization, destructive persistence, private-data boundaries, release authority, new domain invariants or architecture precedents, contradictory intent and incomplete analysis. Routine reversible cases may later qualify for lighter review only after local outcome calibration. Vendor runtime diff limits and model scores are not policy thresholds. No auto-merge or provider action occurs in the prototype.

Ask this PR — a reviewer can interrogate the change

The user's late requirement is part of the proposal: a human reviewer can ask “Did they think of x?” or “How is y implemented?” from the whole PR, a semantic change group, diagram, file or selected line. Keep the question attached to the exact change/spec/architecture baseline, not a fresh unscoped chat. Answers show supporting production code, test assertions and actual runs; separate observed evidence, inference, conflicting evidence and what was not found. Absence of evidence never proves the author did not consider something.

Follow-ups preserve the selected scope and prior question context. A useful explanation can open its sequence/class/ERM slice; an unresolved concern can become a proposed review question, test-gap item or author clarification with its evidence intact. These actions do not authorize execution or grant approval. New head/spec content marks the answer stale and requires reassessment. The mockup accepts arbitrary questions and demonstrates three known fixture answers plus an honest not-established fallback; it does not pretend to call an LLM or inspect the live PR.

Verified documented prior art: GitHub provides PR-, file- and line-scoped Copilot questions and can use the originating agent's session logs for agent-created PRs. The additional obligation links, uncertainty and question-to-backlog workflow here are Inferred AI-DE design. GitHub PR exploration.

Journey 3 — Test coverage and worthiness

Use the same selection with current change / component / whole repository scope. Keep separate: requirement/failure-mode coverage, executed statement/branch coverage, assertion strength, integration fidelity, purposeful versus suspected redundant protection, elapsed/compute cost and reliability. Missing collector data is “Not recorded”; 90% line coverage is never 90% acceptance.

The operator can move from an uncovered criterion to the relevant code and missing boundary case; from a suspected redundant test to overlapping assertions and its platform purpose; from a slow suite to measured setup/runtime breakdown; and from a flaky result to first attempts, retries, environment and observation window. Preserve raw mutant states and review suspected equivalence separately. Quarantine/retries cannot erase failures or create a clean pass rate. Proposed remediation creates backlog entries, not automatic test deletion or weakened gates.

Journey 4 — Codebase understanding at scale

Understand starts with a question and scope: changed component, bounded context, entry point, data entity, or a full-repository grouped overview. A summary graph leads to smaller neighborhoods; the tree/table is an equally capable route. Every view discloses selected/returned/omitted nodes and limits. Search, focus, expand, breadcrumbs and zoom preserve where the user came from.

Choose the representation by question: dependency graph for impact; class/interface for contracts; sequence for an operation and failures; ERM for keys/cardinality/history. Distinguish extracted static evidence, runtime observation and agent inference in the relationship and legend. The source viewer opens from a selected claim or symbol. It is a drill-down, not the default operating surface or a new editable IDE. Unsupported language/ambiguous relation states remain visible.

Journey 5 — Architecture review

Compare accepted constraints and ADRs with the observed component/dependency map. Show one boundary crossing or cycle at a time, its affected criteria, runtime/data consequence and proof. Use before/after overlays for changed dependencies and ERM/schema evolution. Assertions about ownership, bounded contexts or runtime calls carry explicit evidence status.

The view can link a drift to implementation repair, an approved architecture change, an obsolete baseline or an unresolved question. It must not redraw accepted architecture to make a violation disappear. Existing native/static diagrams are reusable; full C4/ERM product surfaces remain new work and must not be counted as shipped because a documentation diagram exists.

Journey 6 — Rigor and risk backlog

One review queue gathers resilience, compliance/policy, privacy, performance and security items with kind risk/issue/todo, priority, affected scope, evidence and disconfirmation, consequence, owner, disposition, dependencies and a falsifiable validation method. Architecture risks join the same evidence model. It reuses existing health incidents and governance artifacts as inputs without pretending those inputs already form an integrated assurance service.

Compliance means evidence against a selected policy/obligation profile, not an AI-generated certification badge. Privacy reviews show actual data boundaries and unknowns; performance recommendations use measured cost axes. The operator may accept a backlog item for planning, dispute it with a reason, or defer it visibly. Those are not production-change authorizations.

Grounded change summaries

An immutable episode packet contains intent and architecture baselines; behavioral delta; production symbols/contracts/schema changes; new or changed tests; actual command/run outcomes; small before/after diagrams; omissions and stale evidence; human decisions; remaining work. Every narrative assertion can open the evidence drawer at the exact artifact version. A model may explain retained facts but cannot promote inferred links or invented measurements into truth.

Success measures and trade-offs

Proposed pilot measures: time to locate required human decisions; time to explain a changed behavior; criteria without meaningful proof; material boundaries not reviewed; stale decisions; first-pass and retry-recovered outcomes; review dispositions and escaped defects; run latency, spend, volume and failures. No productivity multiplier or target percentage is asserted before a baseline exists. Keep human review time and defect outcomes together when calibrating policy.

Main trade-offs: more durable identity and provenance work versus less repeated context assembly; guided summaries versus the risk of hiding unexamined code; compact defaults versus expert access; local authority versus optional provider/network features. The shared drawer and complete-diff escape hatch are controls for those trade-offs, not guarantees that analysis is correct.
